Beans are one of the simplest vegetables to grow — no indoor seed starting, no complicated timing, just direct sow after frost in warm soil. They germinate quickly, grow fast, and produce abundantly. The main thing to get right is soil temperature.

Direct sowing beans

Never start beans indoors — they don't transplant well and grow so quickly from direct sow that there's no advantage. Sow directly in the garden after your last frost date when soil is at least 60°F. Warmer is better: beans in 70°F soil germinate in 6–8 days; in 60°F soil, they take 10–14 days and are more likely to rot before germinating.

Bush beans vs. pole beans

Bush beans grow to 2 feet tall without support, produce all at once over 2–3 weeks, and are done. Great for canning or preserving where you want a large harvest at one time.

Pole beans grow tall (6–10 feet) and need a trellis or support structure. They take a week or two longer to start producing but then produce continuously until frost — much better for fresh eating over the whole season.

Succession planting

For fresh beans all season, succession sow bush beans every 3 weeks. Since each planting produces for only 2–3 weeks, staggered plantings keep beans coming from early summer until the last sowing matures before frost. Pole beans don't need this — a single planting produces all season.

Nitrogen fixation

Beans fix nitrogen from the air into the soil, which benefits subsequent crops. To maximize this, inoculate seeds with rhizobium bacteria before planting (available at garden centers, very cheap). This is optional but produces noticeably better results, especially in soil where beans haven't been grown before.

Common questions

When are beans ready to harvest?

Harvest beans when pods are firm, crisp, and fully sized but before the seeds inside bulge prominently through the pod. At that "pencil-thin" or slightly plumper stage, they're at peak flavor and tenderness. Overripe beans become tough and stringy. Check plants every 2–3 days at peak production.

Can I save bean seeds for next year?

Yes — beans are among the easiest vegetables to save seed from. Leave a few pods on the plant past the eating stage until they're fully dry and brown. Shell the dried beans and store in a cool, dry place. They'll be viable for 3–4 years.

Why aren't my beans producing flowers?

Beans typically flower 5–7 weeks after germination. If they're past that point and not flowering, the most likely causes are excessive nitrogen (from over-fertilizing or very rich soil, which promotes leafy growth over flowering) or heat stress causing flower drop.
